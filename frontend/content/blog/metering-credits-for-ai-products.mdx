The first credit system everyone builds is a counter: one credit per action,
deduct on use. It works until your actions stop costing the same. The day a
single AI call can cost a hundred times another, the flat bucket is
simultaneously too generous and too stingy.

## Rates, not counts

Metered billing charges by tier. A feature declares what it consumes, and the
ledger applies the rate for that model tier:

```typescript
await credits.consume({
  userId,
  featureCode: 'assistant.generate',
  units: tokensUsed,
  modelTier: 'frontier',   // rate looked up per tier
})
```

The same `consume` call still supports the legacy flat form (`{ userId, amount }`)
— metering is additive, not a breaking change.

## Windowed quotas and soft caps

A lifetime total is the wrong unit for a subscription. INITE Billing meters
against a **rolling window** — say 10,000 credits per 30 days — that resets on
renewal:

- **Soft cap** at 90%: the user is warned, nothing breaks.
- **Hard cap**: the consume call returns a quota result instead of charging,
  so your product can degrade gracefully instead of failing.

## Why it matters for AI

If you are billing an AI product on flat credits, you are guessing. Per-tier
rates make the price of a feature track its cost, quotas make spend
predictable, and soft caps keep the experience honest. That is the difference
between a credit system that survives contact with real usage and one that
doesn't.
